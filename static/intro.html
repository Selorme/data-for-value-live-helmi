Tell once. Share selectively.

Helmi is a personal AI agent that talks to businesses’ bots for you. It shares what a place needs to serve you well, holds back what it doesn’t, and asks you when it isn’t sure. Your rules decide what moves; the AI only writes the words.

01 · The everyday problem

Some people explain themselves at every door

Booking a trip means telling the same things to the train, the hotel, the restaurant and the venue. For most people that’s a form. For someone with an access need it is a conversation at every counter, and often a trade: give more of your privacy, or get a worse service.

~1 h

of explaining and waiting

In our scenario, Elina, who is blind and travels with her guide dog Kide, spends about an hour on one weekend trip explaining Kide, having forms read aloud and waiting for help. Everything works out. It just takes longer.

PIN

shared to pay for dog food

A blind woman in Helsinki couldn’t use a touch-screen card terminal, and staff suggested she tell them her PIN. The EU rules cover new terminals from June 2025, but old ones are still allowed. (Yle, 2026)

1 rule

that came out of it

The accessible route must never be the less private route. If someone with an access need has to share more than everyone else, the design has failed, even if they get through in the end.

03 · The idea

Your own agent, with your rules

Your details sit where they already are: your health records, calendar, bank, chats, photos. Helmi can read them only as far as you allow. Businesses send their own bots. The two agents talk, and the code checks every detail before it leaves Helmi.

Share

Helmi just sends it

“Seat with floor space for a guide dog.” Needs like this go out without asking you.

Ask me

Your phone buzzes

“Hotel Merikaari wants your passport number. Share it?” You decide place by place. No answer in 45 seconds means no.

Never

Whatever they offer

Your chats, your search history, your full health record. If an insurer offers 15% off, Helmi still says no.

Share the need, not the record

What a hotel could ask for

What it needs to know

Travels with a guide dog. Information as accessible digital text. Room close to the lift.

05 · How a negotiation goes

Two bots talk. You step in when it matters.

The business’s bot asks

With a reason for each detail, and what it promises about keeping it.

The code checks your rules

Every detail, before anything leaves. The AI never decides what to share.

Helmi answers

Shares needs, holds back records, offers alternatives: “She can show her passport at the desk.”

Your phone buzzes

For anything you marked “Ask me”, place by place.

The business may make an offer

A discount for your health record, a free companion seat for your disability card. You decide again.

Everyone asks separately

Even the restaurant owned by the hotel. Helmi never passes your details from one business to another.

06 · What people chose

People shared less than we expected, and it depended on the place

Our first workshop, 9 November: four participants played trip mode as invented people, then talked it through.

Share of what each place asked for

Event
82%
Hotel
68%
Insurer
14%

3 of 4 shared nothing with the insurer. Nobody let a business read their chats or email.

Let the agent do the legwork, keep the deal

Helmi may search, compare and talk to a car dealer’s bot, but the person sees the car and pays. Buying stocks only inside a limit the person sets. Nobody let Helmi send messages alone.

“Needing access weakens the freedom to say no.”

Participants preferred sharing a need or a verified disability card over a diagnosis.

07 · For businesses

Ask for less, get more

At the stand you run the businesses. Change what a bot asks for and watch what the next traveller shares.

Ask for the need

“Step-free room near the lift” serves the guest. A diagnosis is a liability you now have to protect.

Offer, so people don’t have to explain

Keypad card terminals, an accessible menu, step-free seats on the map. None of them needs any guest data.

Promise only what you keep

Delete at the end of the visit, never pass details to a sister business. Helmi reads these promises.

Keep a person in the loop

A member of staff double-checks access plans and allergies before the guest arrives.
